# Retention and engagement evidence for ~60 s vertical narrated motion-graphics explainers (2025-2026)

Source-type labels used on every finding:
- **[PLATFORM]** published by the platform itself (help pages, official blog, platform marketing-science pages). Platform ad-research numbers are measured on ads, usually commissioned, and not peer reviewed.
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]** journal article, handbook chapter or meta-analysis.
- **[PRACTITIONER]** agency, analytics vendor, blog. Methodology usually unstated; treat as hypothesis.
- **(primary read)** = I read the source text in this session. **(via search summary)** = I only saw a search-engine summary of the page; exact wording unverified.

Research date: 2026-10-07.

## 1. What do the platforms publish about retention signals and the first 1-3 seconds?

### Takeaway
Platforms publish the *names* of the signals (YouTube: viewed vs swiped away and engaged views; Instagram: watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach; TikTok: the first 3-6 seconds) but almost no thresholds; every numeric "good" benchmark in circulation (70-90% viewed-vs-swiped, 60%+ 3-second hold) is practitioner-derived. The only platform-published number on the opening is TikTok's ad-research claim that 90% of ad-recall impact is captured in the first 6 seconds.

### Cited Findings

YouTube
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) YouTube Help defines the Shorts metrics "Shown in Feed" = "the number of times that your Shorts showed in the Shorts Feed" and a report of "the percentage of times that viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away". No target value is given. — [YouTube Help, Content tab analytics tips (Shorts)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en-GB&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) The same help page advises creators to "monitor watch time and audience retention", compare high and low performers, and look for patterns; it does not define average view duration or average percentage viewed for Shorts on that page. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en-GB&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) YouTube Blog, 19 Aug 2026: a view is "now defined as counting from the very first frame — whether that's a Short appearing in the feed or a long-form video autoplaying"; an engaged view is "clicking to watch or watching past the initial seconds". Effective 24 Aug 2026 for all formats. Monetisation and "the vast majority of analytics remain anchored on engaged views", and there is "no change to recommendations". — [YouTube Blog: What are 'Engaged' Views on YouTube?](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)
- [PRACTITIONER reporting a platform change] (via search summary) Shorts views began counting every start or replay, with no minimum watch time, on 31 March 2025; engaged views kept the old definition and still drive Partner Program earnings. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-view-count-update/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/)
- [PLATFORM statement, reported by trade press] (via search summary) Todd Sherman (Shorts product lead), Creator Insider 2023: Shorts viewers swipe through a feed rather than choosing a video; on ideal length he recommended focusing on the story rather than a specific duration; focus on audience over algorithm. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/388588/youtube-shorts-product-lead-offers-insights-for-cr.html)
- [PRACTITIONER] (via search summary) Benchmark in circulation: 70-90% viewed-vs-swiped-away is "good", under 60% performs poorly; origin is a creator-run analysis described as covering 3.3 billion Shorts views. Not a YouTube number. — [Medium, "A Study of 3.3 Billion Views"](https://medium.com/@antoinelacombled/cracking-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-study-of-3-3-billion-views-4711fdf7931b); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/the-creators-guide-to-youtube-shorts-analytics/)

Instagram / Facebook
- [PLATFORM statement, secondary paraphrase only] (primary read of the secondary article; it contains no direct Mosseri quotes) Adam Mosseri named three top signals in January 2025: watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach; likes weigh slightly more for followers, sends slightly more for non-followers. — [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance); [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-algorithm/)
- [PRACTITIONER] (via search summary) "Sends count 3-5x more than likes" is a community estimate; Meta has not confirmed a ratio. — [socialday.live](https://socialday.live/features/instagrams-dm-share-signal-now-drives-3-to-5-times-more-reach-than-likes)
- [PRACTITIONER] (via search summary) "Reels with 60%+ 3-second hold outperform weak holds by 5-10x in reach" and "up to 50% of viewers drop in the first three seconds": no methodology found. — [creatorflow.so](https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026/)
- [PRACTITIONER reporting platform policy] (via search summary) Reels longer than 3 minutes are not recommended in Explore/Reels tab; Instagram's in-app "Best Practices" feature recommends a strong hook, captions, audio, non-watermarked original content. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-algorithm/)

TikTok
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) "90% of ad recall impact is captured within the first 6 seconds" (TikTok Creative Guide: Driving Brand Equity, 2020). — [TikTok for Business, Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) TikTok's SMB playbook: "The first 3-6 seconds are critical"; "regardless of format, the first six seconds are vital for impact"; supported lengths 15 s to 3 min, "any video length within this range can find success"; full-screen 9:16; not below 720p. — [TikTok Creative Made Simple (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- [PRACTITIONER citing TikTok] (via search summary) "63% of videos with the highest CTR hook within the first 3 seconds", attributed to TikTok for Business; I did not locate the primary page. — [HeyOrca](https://www.heyorca.com/blog/best-tiktok-hooks)
- [PRACTITIONER] (via search summary) "TikTok makes its initial distribution call around 1.5 s" and "completion rate is 40-50% of the ranking decision": unsourced estimates. — [Hansen Commerce](https://hansencommerce.com/insights-tiktok-hook-3-seconds)

### Inferences
- The consistent cross-platform, platform-level message is: the decision to stay happens in the opening seconds (YouTube measures it as a binary view-vs-swipe; TikTok says 3-6 s), and sustained watch time plus shares are what the feeds reward. None publishes a threshold, so thresholds should be set from the channel's own analytics, not from blog benchmarks.
- Since YouTube now counts a view at frame 1 and counts replays, raw view counts are inflated relative to pre-2025; "engaged views" and viewed-vs-swiped are the metrics to track.
- For a 60 s explainer, completion-style metrics will be structurally lower than for 15 s clips; compare against own-channel videos of similar length.

### Gaps
- No platform-published numeric benchmark for viewed-vs-swiped, average percentage viewed, or 3-second hold was found.
- I could not verify a direct Mosseri quote or Instagram's official creators page text; all Instagram items are secondary paraphrase.
- No Facebook Reels-specific official guidance was retrieved; Meta guidance is assumed shared with Instagram but that is unverified.
- No official statement was found that saves, replays or loops are weighted in a specific way.

## 2. Hooks: which structures are supported, and how long?

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed or platform data was found that compares hook *types* (question vs surprising result vs problem-first) for educational shorts; the supported claims are only that the opening 3-6 s carry most of the effect and that a hook must be paid off. Hook-type rankings are practitioner folklore.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) TikTok's playbook lists hook templates (problem/solution tip, call out the audience, list of N reasons, popularity proof) and states "Every hook needs a payoff. Follow your hook with key selling points and close out with a strong call-to-action". No performance comparison between templates is given. — [TikTok Creative Made Simple (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) 90% of ad-recall impact in the first 6 seconds (2020 ad study). — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) "Faster scene changes draw viewers early" (TikTok Marketing Science Global Information Density study, 2022, Neuro-Insight), i.e. the opening benefits from visible change. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)
- [PRACTITIONER] (via search summary) Advice to give sound-off scrollers a text overlay and sound-on viewers a spoken line in the hook, and to resolve the hook by the 3 s mark. — [HeyOrca](https://www.heyorca.com/blog/best-tiktok-hooks); [Hansen Commerce](https://hansencommerce.com/insights-tiktok-hook-3-seconds)
- [PRACTITIONER] (via search summary) Ad-industry "hook rate" (3-second plays / impressions): ~25% average, 30-40% target, 40%+ elite on Meta ads. This is a paid-ads metric with impressions as denominator and is not comparable to organic viewed-vs-swiped. — [sepia-lab](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/hook-rate-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- A defensible spec for a 60 s explainer: state the question or surprising outcome in on-screen text and voice within the first 3 s, with something already moving at frame 1, and make sure the promised answer is actually delivered later (TikTok's "every hook needs a payoff").
- For beginners, a problem-first or question hook is consistent with learning science indirectly (it activates a goal for the explanation), but I found no direct experimental test, so treat hook type as an A/B variable.

### Gaps
- No controlled or large-sample study comparing hook structures for educational short video was found.
- No evidence on optimal hook length finer than "first 3-6 s".
- Everything here is ad-derived or anecdotal; none is specific to faceless motion graphics.

## 3. Pacing: how often should something change; evidence for or against very fast pacing

### Takeaway
"Change something every 2-3 seconds" is practitioner advice, with only a vague platform statement behind it (faster scene changes help early). Learning science points the other way for novices: system-paced, complex material overloads beginners, so changes should carry meaning (segment boundaries, cues) rather than be decorative.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) TikTok: "Faster scene changes draw viewers early and increase impression chances" (Neuro-Insight, 2022). No rate is specified. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)
- [PRACTITIONER] (via search summary) "For ads under 30 seconds, scene changes should occur every 2-3 seconds". No data shown. — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-creative-center-best-practices)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) Segmenting meta-analysis (Rey et al., 2019; 56 investigations, 88 comparisons): presenting material in meaningful segments gives "small to medium effects for retention and transfer", reduces cognitive load, and the effects are "confirmed for a system-paced segmentation" (i.e. designer-inserted breaks, not only learner-clicked ones). Learners with high prior knowledge benefitted more on retention. — [Rey et al. 2019, Educational Psychology Review](https://maria-wirzberger.de/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Rey2019_Article_AMeta-analysisOfTheSegmentingE.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (via search summary) Mayer's segmenting principle: median d = 0.67 in 7 of 7 tests (older, smaller evidence base than Rey et al.). — [Mayer, Multimedia Instruction](https://eddl.tru.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/mayer-multimedia-instruction.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) Coherence effect is stronger "when the lesson was system-paced rather than learner-paced" and for low working-memory learners (Rey, 2012, cited in Mayer & Fiorella): extraneous material hurts most when the viewer cannot slow down. — [Mayer & Fiorella 2014, Cambridge Handbook ch. 12](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (via search summary) Guo, Kim & Rubin (2014), 6.9 million edX video sessions: video length was the strongest predictor of engagement; recommendation of 6 minutes or less; Khan-style drawing (visuals that build continuously) more engaging than static slides. — [UBC summary](https://blogs.ubc.ca/videolearninghub/2019/08/04/how-video-production-affects-student-engagement-an-empirical-study-of-mooc-videos/); [KAIST record](https://pure.kaist.ac.kr/en/publications/how-video-production-affects-student-engagement-an-empirical-stud/)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (via search summary) Playback-speed experiment (n = 76): learning was best at 1.25x-1.5x and fell at 2x, with cognitive load rising with speed. Evidence that moderate speed-up is tolerated and extreme is not; it concerns lecture video, not shorts. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.839982/full)

### Inferences
- Reconcile the two bodies of evidence by making frequent visible change *informational*: each change should be a new step of the explanation, a cue, or a camera move to the next idea. That satisfies "something changes" for retention and "segmented, cued, coherent" for learning.
- For a 60 s beginner explainer, roughly 6-10 segments of one idea each with a brief visual beat between them is consistent with the segmenting evidence; a fixed cuts-per-10-seconds number is not evidence-backed.
- Guo et al.'s finding that continuously building visuals beat static slides supports progressive drawing/reveals synchronized to narration over static frames with a voice on top.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that tests cut rate or scene length in short-form educational video against both retention (watch time) and learning.
- The TikTok scene-change statement has no published rate, sample or effect size.
- Guo et al.'s speaking-rate result (faster speakers more engaging) and exact median engagement figures were not verified in this session.

## 4. Captions: watch time, comprehension, karaoke vs sentence captions, sound-off share

### Takeaway
Survey evidence says many people watch muted and say captions keep them watching; platform ad research says text overlays raise view time. For learning, verbatim full-sentence captions on top of narrated animation are the classic redundancy case (harmful for novices), while short keyword text is neutral-to-helpful; the one study found on time-synchronized highlighted captions calls them "useful but distracting".

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER survey, industry-commissioned] (via search summary) Verizon Media / Publicis Media 2019, n = 5,616 US consumers: 69% watch video with sound off in public places; 80% say they are more likely to watch a whole video when captions are available; 37% say captions encourage them to turn sound on; captioned ads +8% ad recall, +10% ad memory quality, +13% brand linkage. Self-report, not behavioural watch-time data. — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tjmccue/2019/07/31/verizon-media-says-69-percent-of-consumers-watching-video-with-sound-off/); [3Play Media](https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/); [Marketing Charts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/digital/video-108656)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) TikTok: text overlays "increase view time, drive recall, and boost likability" (TikTok Marketing Science US SMB Creative Effectiveness Study, 2021, Lumen). No magnitude given. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)
- [PLATFORM] (via search summary, wording unverified) TikTok Creative Center material: adding captions boosts view time, affinity, likability, recall; "displaying 5-10 words/sec" described as a sweet spot with a "+2.1x awareness lift". This rate is implausibly fast if read as reading speed and may be a mis-summary; verify before use. — [TikTok Creative Center, Power of Creative Elements](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/Power_Creative_Elements/pc/en)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) 88% of TikTok users say sound is vital to the TikTok experience (Kantar, 2022); TikTok's playbook: "To capture attention, videos should always include sound." This contradicts applying the 69% sound-off figure to TikTok. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads); [TikTok playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- [PRACTITIONER] (via search summary) "Adding captions increases completion rate by 32%": no source or method given. — [Akselera](https://akselera.tech/en/insights/guides/tiktok-hooks-captions-strategy)
- [PEER-REVIEWED, HCI conference paper] (primary read) "Useful but Distracting" (within-subjects, n = 49, second-language viewers, 2-3 min film clips): standard captions ranked first for comprehension and entertainment; full captions with time-synchronized keyword highlights ranked first only when the goal was learning; highlights caused attention-switching and "a majority of users still preferred standard captions". Vocabulary recall showed no significant differences (ceiling: 88% pre-test). — [arXiv 2307.05870](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05870); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3701571.3701574)
- [PRACTITIONER / accessibility advocacy] (via search summary) Word-by-word captions that break phrase boundaries are argued to raise reading effort and disadvantage slower readers. — [Disabled World](https://www.disabled-world.com/disability/accessibility/karaoke-captions.php)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] See section 5 (redundancy) for the learning effect of verbatim on-screen text with narration.

### Inferences
- Captions serve two different goals that pull apart: retention/accessibility (keep them, especially on Instagram/Facebook where muted viewing is common) and learning (avoid a full verbatim sentence competing with the animation).
- The compromise consistent with both: short phrase-group captions (a few words at a time, broken at phrase boundaries) in a fixed zone away from the graphic, with at most a single-word emphasis colour. This keeps on-screen text short (redundancy boundary condition), avoids single-word karaoke flicker, and still serves muted viewers.
- The 69% sound-off number is a 2019 "in public places" survey figure and should not be quoted as the share of Shorts/TikTok viewers watching muted.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or platform study was found directly comparing word-by-word (karaoke) captions against full-sentence captions on watch time or comprehension for native-language short video.
- No current (2025-2026) platform-published sound-off share for Shorts, Reels or TikTok was found.
- No behavioural (non-survey) measure of caption effect on organic short-video watch time with stated methodology was found.

## 5. Mayer's multimedia principles for narrated animation, effect sizes, and the redundancy boundary conditions

### Takeaway
For narrated animation the best-supported design rules are: synchronize narration with the matching visual, cut everything not needed, cue what to look at, break into segments, speak conversationally in a human voice, and do not duplicate the narration as full on-screen sentences. The redundancy effect is large in Mayer's lab tests (median d = 0.86) but is eliminated or reversed when the on-screen text is short (a keyword or phrase beside the graphic), when learners are experienced, or when there are no graphics.

### Cited Findings
Effect sizes below are from Mayer & Fiorella's handbook chapter (primary read) unless stated; they are medians of mostly short, lab, system-paced lessons with college students on transfer tests, and independent meta-analyses usually report smaller values.

- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) **Coherence**: exclude extraneous words, sounds and pictures. Supported 23/23 tests, median d = 0.86. Rey (2012) meta-analysis: d = 0.65 for excluding extraneous text, d = 0.83 for extraneous illustrations (transfer). Stronger for low working-memory learners, system-paced lessons, and highly distracting extras. — [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) **Signaling/cueing**: add cues that highlight the organisation of essential material. Supported 24/28 tests, median d = 0.41 (verbal signals d = 0.50; visual cues d = 0.36, stronger for spreading colour, weaker for arrows). Visual cues work best when they guide attention "both spatially and temporally" (de Koning et al., 2009). Stronger for low-knowledge learners, when used "sparingly rather than excessively", and for complex displays. — [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (via search summary of abstract) Signaling meta-analysis, Schneider et al. 2018: 103 studies, N = 12,201; retention g+ = 0.53 (95% CI 0.42-0.64), transfer g+ = 0.33 (95% CI 0.22-0.43). — [Educational Research Review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1747938X17300581)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) **Redundancy**: people learn more deeply from graphics + narration than from graphics + narration + on-screen text, where narration and on-screen text are *identical*. Supported 16/16 tests, median d = 0.86 on transfer. — [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) **Redundancy boundary conditions**: "the redundancy effect can be eliminated or even reversed when the learners are experienced, the on-screen text is short, or the material lacks graphics."
  - Short text: Mayer & Johnson (2008) added "a key word or phrase printed next to the corresponding part of the graphic"; the keyword group did *better* on retention (Exp. 1 d = 0.47; Exp. 2 d = 0.70) with no difference on transfer (d = 0.04; d = 0.15). Rationale given: a few words "may not be enough to distract the learner from the graphic and may even signal where to look".
  - Expertise: low-experience learners show the redundancy effect, high-experience learners do not (Kalyuga, Chandler & Sweller 1999, 2000).
  - No graphics: Adesope & Nesbit (2012) found spoken + printed beat spoken alone when there were no graphics (d = 0.24) but not with concurrent animation (d = 0.06).
  - Second language: redundancy can hurt when verbal material is lengthy (Diao & Sweller, 2006).
  — [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (via search summary) Adesope & Nesbit 2012 meta-analysis (57 independent studies): the redundancy effect is "small and heavily dependent on" pacing, learner characteristics and design; beneficial conditions include no graphics, slow or learner pacing, technical/unfamiliar words, and added text shorter than the narration; narration with short written keywords fosters learning while identical written text impairs it. — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232469670_Verbal_Redundancy_in_Multimedia_Learning_Environments_A_Meta-Analysis); [Frontiers 2023 discussion](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1148035/full)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (via search summary) Learners misjudge this: a study titled "Learners misperceive the benefits of redundant text in multimedia learning" exists (PubMed 25071674). — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25071674/)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) **Temporal contiguity**: present corresponding animation and narration simultaneously, not successively. Supported 9/9 tests, median d = 1.22; Ginns (2006) meta-analysis mean d = 0.78 across 13 experiments. Boundary conditions: weaker for low-spatial-ability learners; negligible under learner control (must be "fast-paced and under system control" to matter); eliminated or reversed when material is presented in small chunks within working-memory capacity; strongest for complex material. — [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) **Spatial contiguity**: place words near the corresponding part of the graphic. Supported 22/22 tests, median d = 1.10. — [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) **Segmenting**: Rey et al. 2019 meta-analysis, 56 investigations / 88 comparisons, small-to-medium effects on retention and transfer, lower cognitive load; holds for system-paced segmentation. — [Rey et al. 2019](https://maria-wirzberger.de/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Rey2019_Article_AMeta-analysisOfTheSegmentingE.pdf). Mayer's own summary: median d = 0.67, 7/7 tests (via search summary). — [Mayer, Multimedia Instruction](https://eddl.tru.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/mayer-multimedia-instruction.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (via search summary) **Modality**: graphics + narration beat graphics + on-screen text; Ginns (2005) meta-analysis found a moderate-to-large average effect for complex, system-paced materials and smaller effects for self-paced or simple materials. — [Ginns-related record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475211000570)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (via search summary) **Personalization**: conversational rather than formal wording; supported 14/17 tests, median d = 0.79. **Voice**: human rather than machine voice; 5/6 tests, median d = 0.74. **Embodiment**: 11/11, median d = 0.36 (applies to on-screen agents, not relevant to faceless video). — [Mayer, Cambridge Handbook ch. 14](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-based-on-social-cues-in-multimedia-learning-personalization-voice-image-and-embodiment-principles/3841340D8AD820C26DBCD39AE664BCEC)

### Inferences
- Answer to the specific question: showing the *same full sentence* as on-screen text while it is narrated over an animation is exactly the condition the redundancy principle warns against for novices. Showing a *few key words*, placed near the relevant graphic or in short groups, falls inside the documented boundary condition and behaves like signaling (retention benefit d ≈ 0.5-0.7 in two experiments, no transfer cost).
- A 60 s short is system-paced, fast, and aimed at beginners: the conditions under which coherence, redundancy, temporal contiguity and signaling effects are *largest*. So tight voice-to-motion sync and stripping decoration matter more here than in a self-paced course video.
- Segment-sized chunks reduce the penalty for small sync offsets (temporal contiguity boundary condition), but sync still should be on the word that names the element.
- The voice principle's evidence predates modern neural TTS; whether a high-quality neural voice counts as "machine" is untested in the sources read. Treat TTS as a risk to check, not a proven harm.
- Use cues sparingly: one highlight per idea (the evidence says excessive signaling loses the effect).

### Gaps
- Could not access Noetel et al. (2022, Review of Educational Research) meta-meta-analysis or the 2025 Educational Research Review meta-analysis of Mayer's research (both returned 403); these would give the most current pooled effect sizes and should be consulted.
- Adesope & Nesbit's abstract, participant count and exact moderator g values were not verified at source.
- Exact pooled g values from Rey et al. (2019) were not extracted (abstract states only "small to medium").
- No study tests these principles on sub-90-second vertical feed video specifically; all transfer to shorts is inference.
- No peer-reviewed evidence on modern neural TTS versus human narration was retrieved.

## 6. Endings and loops: loopable endings, payoff, late CTA

### Takeaway
Almost nothing is established. Platforms confirm replays count as views (YouTube) and TikTok ad research reports CTA cards lifting recall and likeability; the claim that seamless loops raise distribution is practitioner folklore with no published test.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM change, reported by practitioners] (via search summary) Since 31 March 2025 a Shorts view is counted "whenever a Short starts or replays", so loops inflate views; engaged views are separate. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-view-count-update/)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) YouTube: recommendations unchanged by the view-definition change; analytics remain anchored on engaged views. So counting replays as views is not evidence that loops are rewarded. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) TikTok: "CTA cards drive 45% lift in recall and 19% increase in likeability" (TikTok Marketing Science Top-Performing BLS Creative Analysis, 2020). Ads, and the CTA card is an ad format element, not a spoken end-line. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) TikTok playbook: "End with a clear CTA"; "Every hook needs a payoff". — [TikTok Creative Made Simple (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- [PRACTITIONER] (via search summary) Advice to "loop [Reels] to stretch viewing time"; secondary paraphrase that Instagram watch time includes replays. No data. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-algorithm/); [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance)

### Inferences
- A CTA in the last seconds gives viewers a visible "it's over" signal, which plausibly causes swipes before 100%; keeping the payoff late and the CTA short (and visually continuous with the content) limits that, but this is reasoning, not data.
- For follows, a late CTA only reaches viewers who stayed, so its effect is bounded by retention to that point; test end-card length (e.g. 2 s vs 4 s) on own analytics.

### Gaps
- No platform or peer-reviewed measurement of loopable endings on retention, reach or follows.
- No data on CTA placement (end vs mid) for organic educational shorts or on follow conversion.
- No evidence on end-payoff structures ("answer at the end") versus early-answer structures for retention.

## 7. Sound: music level, sound effects, measured effects

### Takeaway
Platform research says sound matters for attention on TikTok; controlled learning studies say irrelevant background music and environmental sounds *reduce* learning from narrated animation (d = 0.51 to 1.49 in the classic experiments). No source gave a measured optimal music level or a measured engagement effect of sound effects on key moments.

### Cited Findings
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) 88% of TikTok users say sound is vital to the experience (Kantar, 2022); "half of users say music makes content more uplifting and entertaining" (2020). Perception surveys. — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)
- [PLATFORM] (primary read) "To capture attention, videos should always include sound." — [TikTok Creative Made Simple (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- [PRACTITIONER] (via search summary) "Trending audio boosts reach by 48%": unsourced. — [Akselera](https://akselera.tech/en/insights/guides/tiktok-hooks-captions-strategy)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) Moreno & Mayer (2000): adding background music and environmental sounds to narrated animation "resulted in poorer transfer test performance"; effect sizes favouring the no-music/no-sound version d = 1.49 (lightning, Exp. 1) and d = 0.51 (brakes, Exp. 2). — [Mayer & Fiorella 2014, Table 12.4](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (primary read) Counter-result: Doolittle & Altstaedter (2009) found added extraneous sounds and animations "did not significantly hurt transfer", possibly because they were not distracting enough. — [Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] (via search summary) Sundararajan & Adesope (2020) seductive-details meta-analysis: seductive details hinder learning, moderated by format and learner pacing. Studies on background music in instructional video exist with mixed results. — [Educational Psychology Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4); [ERIC EJ1393962](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1393962.pdf)

### Inferences
- The two bodies of evidence measure different outcomes (attention/liking versus learning). A reasonable reconciliation for a beginner explainer: keep a music bed low and unobtrusive (or absent under dense explanation) and use sound effects only where they mark a meaningful event, where they function as an auditory signal rather than decoration.
- The Moreno & Mayer experiments used music *and* environmental sounds that were irrelevant to the content; they do not show that a sound synchronized to a key visual event is harmful. That case is untested in what I read.

### Gaps
- No measured recommendation for music level (dB/LUFS relative to voice) from platforms or peer-reviewed sources.
- No measured engagement or learning effect for sound effects placed on key moments.
- No A/B data on music versus no music for organic educational shorts retention.
