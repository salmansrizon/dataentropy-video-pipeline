# Cue the spoken word, not the scene

The best-supported way to make these videos more engaging and more human is to stop timing motion to scene starts and start timing it to words: when the voice names a column, a keyword or a result, that one element is cued within about two frames of the word, and nothing else moves. Learning research backs this strongly. Signalling what the narration refers to improves retention (g+ = 0.53), simultaneous narration and animation beats misaligned presentation (median d = 1.22), and decorative motion measurably harms recall. All three voices can supply the word timings on a CPU: Kokoro and the Coqui VITS model expose their own predicted durations, and Chatterbox output can be force-aligned against the known script. Emotion then has to be carried by tempo, colour saturation and the number of things changing, because the brand rules out the bounce, shake and scale emphasis that most emotive-motion research relies on. A presenter or lip-synced face is the least supported option (adding a speaker's image has an effect of d = 0.07 and draws 30 to 40% of gaze), while conversational wording and a human-sounding voice show effects near d = 0.8. Platform guidance adds only that the first 3 to 6 seconds decide whether a viewer stays. No source tests any of this on 60-second vertical explainers, so every transfer to this format is inference and should be checked against the channel's own analytics. Nothing in this report was installed or run.

Three labels mark the evidence throughout. **Strong** means peer-reviewed experiments or meta-analyses, a standard, or source code read directly. **Platform/practitioner** means platform ad research, help pages, design-system guidance or blog advice. **Inference** means the researchers' own reasoning or arithmetic.

## Signalling in step with narration is the one lever with strong evidence

The learning literature on narrated animation is the firmest ground available, and a 60-second beginner explainer sits where its effects are largest: system-paced, fast, and aimed at novices. **Signalling** (cues that direct attention to the element being discussed) was supported in 24 of 28 tests with a median d = 0.41, works best when cues guide attention "both spatially and temporally", and is stronger for low-knowledge learners and when used "sparingly rather than excessively" ([Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)). A meta-analysis of 103 studies puts the effect at **g+ = 0.53 for retention and 0.33 for transfer** ([Schneider et al. 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1747938X17300581)), and a second finds d = 0.38 with text-based signals outperforming picture-based ones ([Alpizar et al. 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-020-09748-7)). **Temporal contiguity** (narration and matching visual at the same time) was supported in 9 of 9 tests with a median d = 1.22, though that compares simultaneous with fully successive presentation and therefore justifies sync in principle, not a frame tolerance ([Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)).

The same body of work says what to remove. Cutting extraneous material was supported in 23 of 23 tests (median d = 0.86), and the penalty for extras is larger when the viewer cannot slow the lesson down ([Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)). Decorative animation specifically impaired recall and added extraneous load ([Advances in Physiology Education](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/advan.00102.2019)). Background music and environmental sounds added to narrated animation lowered transfer scores in the classic experiments (d = 1.49 and 0.51), although one later study found no significant harm ([Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)). The brand's limit of three focal points is therefore not only a style rule: every moving element that is not the thing being spoken about is a candidate distraction (inference from the above).

Platform evidence on engagement is thinner and of a different kind. YouTube reports the share of viewers who "viewed your Shorts versus swiped away" but publishes no target value ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en-GB&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts)), and its analytics "remain anchored on engaged views" now that a view counts from the first frame ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)). TikTok's ad research says "90% of ad recall impact is captured within the first 6 seconds" and that "faster scene changes draw viewers early", with no rate given ([TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)); its playbook adds that "every hook needs a payoff" ([TikTok Creative Made Simple](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)). The widely repeated numeric benchmarks (70 to 90% viewed-versus-swiped, a change every 2 to 3 seconds) are practitioner figures without published method ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/the-creators-guide-to-youtube-shorts-analytics/); [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-creative-center-best-practices)). No study was found comparing hook types, cut rates or loopable endings for educational shorts.

The two bodies of evidence reconcile if frequent change is made informational (inference). Presenting material in meaningful segments gives small-to-medium gains even when the designer, not the learner, sets the breaks ([Rey et al. 2019](https://maria-wirzberger.de/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Rey2019_Article_AMeta-analysisOfTheSegmentingE.pdf)). So a 60-second video of roughly six to ten one-idea segments, each built by cues that land on the words naming them, gives the feed its visible change and the learner a coherent explanation. A fixed changes-per-ten-seconds target has no evidence behind it.

## All three voices can deliver word timings on a CPU

The current pipeline stores one duration per scene and no per-word data, so word-level cueing starts in the voiceover step. The route differs by voice, and each is CPU-only.

| Voice | Source of word timings | Resolution | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kokoro-82M (English) | `tokens[].start_ts` / `end_ts` on each `KPipeline.Result`, from the model's own duration predictor | 12.5 ms grid | English only; start offset is a flagged heuristic; accuracy unpublished |
| Chatterbox (English) | None returned; force-align the finished WAV against the known script | Aligner-dependent | Sampling makes timing differ on every run |
| Coqui VITS (Bangla) | `durations` in the dict returned by `Vits.inference()` | About 11.6 ms per frame at default config | Not available through the high-level API; checkpoint config not inspected |

Kokoro fills the timestamps only in its English branches, places word boundaries on a 12.5 ms grid, and carries the author's own comment "TODO: Is -3 an appropriate offset?" on the start position ([kokoro pipeline.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hexgrad/kokoro/main/kokoro/pipeline.py)). Timestamps are relative to each result chunk, punctuation is its own token, and any later tempo change or silence trim on the WAV invalidates them (inference from the source). Chatterbox's `generate()` has no timestamp option ([chatterbox tts.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/master/src/chatterbox/tts.py)). VITS computes a frame count per input token and returns it alongside the audio, with default `hop_length = 256` and `sample_rate = 22050` ([coqui-ai/TTS vits.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/coqui-ai/TTS/dev/TTS/tts/models/vits.py)); because its duration predictor is stochastic, audio and durations must be saved from the same call.

For forced alignment, the only head-to-head benchmark found, on human read speech, puts Montreal Forced Aligner at **72.8% of word boundaries within 25 ms**, WhisperX at 52.7% and MMS at 43.5%, with all three above 94% within 100 ms ([Rousso et al., Interspeech 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19363)). MFA has no Bengali model and installs through conda ([MFA models](https://mfa-models.readthedocs.io/en/latest/acoustic/index.html)). The pip-installable option that covers both languages is ctc-forced-aligner, whose default model is licensed CC-BY-NC 4.0 ([ctc-forced-aligner README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MahmoudAshraf97/ctc-forced-aligner/main/README.md)); whether timing a commercial video with it counts as commercial use is a question the sources do not settle. stable-ts aligns known text in one call but its repository states development is "indefinitely paused" ([stable-ts README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jianfch/stable-ts/main/README.md)). No accuracy figure exists for any tool on Bengali or on synthetic speech.

On the Remotion side nothing new is needed. `@remotion/captions` is already installed at 4.0.529 and defines a `Caption` word shape (`text`, `startMs`, `endMs`) plus `createTikTokStyleCaptions()` to page words into groups; both are pure functions that any Python step can feed through a JSON file ([Remotion captions API](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/captions/api); [createTikTokStyleCaptions](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/captions/create-tiktok-style-captions)). The researchers' proposal (inference, untested) is one JSON file per scene WAV in that shape, stamped with the WAV's hash so the build fails when audio is regenerated without re-timing, and a single helper that turns "word n of scene i" into a frame for the existing `kf`/`enter` helpers. Marking cue words in the voiceover text, as the brief already does with `*word*`, is sturdier than word indices when copy changes.

How tight the sync must be has a standard behind it, though for lip-sync rather than graphics. ITU-R BT.1359-1 puts detectability at about +45 ms when sound is early and −125 ms when sound is late, and acceptability at +90 ms to −185 ms ([ITU-R BT.1359-1](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1359-1-199811-I!!PDF-E.pdf)); a speech-perception study finds the same asymmetry ([van Wassenhove et al. 2007](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002839320600011X?via=ihub%3D)). Translated to 30 fps, a cue that trails the word becomes noticeable after about 1.3 frames, while a cue that leads goes unnoticed up to about 3.7 frames. The working rule (inference) is to **start the cue two frames before the word's onset, never more than one frame after it**, and to anchor the start of an eased entrance to the word, not its end. The frame arithmetic assumes a 30 fps composition, which was not checked.

## Tempo, saturation and element count are the emotional dials the brand leaves open

Research on motion and affect points to tempo as the main dial. Faster motion raises perceived arousal and urgency, smooth continuous paths read as calm and positive, and sharp angular movement reads as tense; higher colour saturation also raises arousal ([Emotion-Aware Design review, arXiv 2502.16038](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16038v3)). The link between speed and positive or negative feeling is contested ([arXiv 2012.08849](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.08849)), and the specific numbers in the review were relayed second-hand, so only the directions are used here. Kinetic-typography research shows animated text can reliably convey tone of voice ([Lee, Forlizzi & Hudson 2002](http://johnnylee.net/kt/dist/files/Kinetic_Typography.pdf)), but its vocabulary of shaking, bouncing, jitter, flashing and size jumps is exactly what the brand forbids. What survives is fade, colour, weight and timing; how much expressive range is lost by that restriction is unmeasured.

Design systems supply a brand-compatible structure for the remaining range. IBM Carbon splits motion into a "productive" register that stays "subtle and out of the way" and an "expressive" one reserved for significant moments, with no overshoot in either set of curves and durations from 70 to 700 ms ([IBM Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/)). Material's tokens run from 50 to 1000 ms with the rule that "duration should increase as the area/traversal of an animation increases" ([Material Components Android](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-components-android/master/docs/theming/Motion.md)). Mapped to narration (inference), calm explanatory lines take the quiet register, and the hook, a stressed word and the payoff take the expressive one. The brand curve `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` already belongs to the expressive family and is currently used for everything that enters, which leaves the payoff no louder register to move into. Adding a quieter register for routine reveals creates that headroom.

The emotional arc below is the researchers' synthesis; no empirical source prescribes an intensity curve for a 60-second explainer.

| Beat | Voice | Motion register (inference) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Question or surprising result | One focal point, one quick smooth reveal then a hold, saturated violet on the key word |
| Problem | Rising tension | Shorter durations, tighter stagger, more of the scene dimmed; paths stay smooth |
| Explanation | Calm, steady | Slowest tempo, lowest saturation, camera nearly still, one cue per spoken term |
| Payoff | Emphatic | The single largest camera move or fullest colour, then a hold |
| CTA | Settled | Static, one focal point |

Working values at 30 fps, derived from the Carbon and Material ladders, are 5 to 12 frames for a calm transition, 12 to 21 frames for an emphasised one, 21 to 30 frames or more for a large camera move, about 21 frames to dim surroundings, and 3 to 6 frames for a colour-only word highlight. Stagger of 2 to 4 frames between sibling rows and holding still through spoken pauses have no numeric source and are suggestions to tune by eye. These calm-register values sit below the 12-frame floor for entrances in the project's existing motion-review policy, so adopting them means a deliberate, versioned policy change, not an adjustment made during a review.

Two conflicts need a decision. The workspace's documented convention (cards fade and rise 24 px, elements grow from 0.96 scale) contradicts the brand limit that elements must not appear by scaling or rising. The compliant vocabulary is opacity fade, colour change, masked wipe, line-draw, dimming of surroundings and whole-scene camera moves. A camera push-in scales the whole scene, not one element; the researchers read it as compliant, but it warrants brand sign-off. Classic anticipation and exaggeration also read as a pop, and the brand-safe substitute (inference) is a non-spatial pre-cue: begin dimming the surroundings or drifting the camera 6 to 10 frames before the element changes.

Driving visuals from the voice's loudness has tooling but no evidence. Remotion's `@remotion/media-utils` offers per-frame audio data ([Remotion audio visualization](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/audio/visualization)), yet no study was found on whether a voice-reactive element helps or distracts, and speech at four to six syllables per second puts a syllable-tracking pulse close to rapid flashing. It belongs last on the list, limited to a heavily smoothed glow on the element being discussed.

## The human feel comes from script, voice and captions, not a face

The evidence on what makes narration feel like a person is lopsided. Conversational wording was supported in 14 of 17 tests with a median d = 0.79, and a human voice over a machine voice in 5 of 6 with d = 0.74 ([Cambridge Handbook ch. 14](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-based-on-social-cues-in-multimedia-learning-personalization-voice-image-and-embodiment-principles/3841340D8AD820C26DBCD39AE664BCEC)), though the voice finding predates neural TTS and published work questions whether it still holds ([ERIC EJ1341358](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1341358.pdf)). By contrast, adding the speaker's image shows **d = 0.07** ([meta-analysis of Mayer's research](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1747938X25000673)), a visible instructor reduces dwell time on the relevant content ([instructor presence meta-analyses](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1747938X2300057X)), and viewers spend around 41% of the time on the face ([Kizilcec et al.](https://vhil.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj29011/files/media/file/kizilcec-jep-instructors-face.pdf)). Agents help only when they do something useful: simple 2D agents reach g+ = 0.38 against 0.11 for 3D ones ([Castro-Alonso et al. 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1310158)), and gesturing instructors reach g = 0.28 to 0.31 ([Educational Psychology Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-024-09910-0)). Videos showing only the instructor's hand were marginally favoured, which suggests a pointer or highlight that moves with the narration is a better use of motion than a face (inference). Making the voices themselves expressive is covered in the earlier report at `D:\vedio generation\reports\Expressive human sounding TTS voices.md`; its script, pause and per-line speed settings are the audio half of this plan.

If a character is ever wanted, the feasible route on this PC is a flat 2D mouth swapped per frame. Rhubarb Lip Sync is MIT-licensed, ships a Windows binary, and turns a WAV into six to nine timed mouth shapes ([rhubarb-lip-sync](https://github.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync)). Realistic talking heads are not practical: Wav2Lip is non-commercial ([Wav2Lip](https://github.com/Rudrabha/Wav2Lip)) and the permissively licensed models are built for GPUs. A photoreal avatar also triggers YouTube's disclosure rule for realistic synthetic content, while fully animated video is exempt ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). Secondary sources say Meta's rule also covers "realistic-sounding audio", which could reach a TTS voiceover with or without a presenter; that wording needs checking on Meta's own pages.

Captions are where voice and picture meet most visibly, and here learning and retention pull in different directions. Identical on-screen sentences alongside narrated graphics lowered learning in 16 of 16 tests (median d = 0.86), but the effect disappears or reverses when the text is short: a keyword beside the graphic improved retention (d = 0.47 and 0.70) with no transfer cost ([Mayer & Fiorella 2014](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)). A within-subjects study of time-synchronised highlighted captions found them "useful but distracting", with most users preferring standard captions ([arXiv 2307.05870](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05870)). On the retention side, TikTok's ad research says text overlays "increase view time" without giving a magnitude ([TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)), and the often-quoted 69% sound-off figure is a 2019 survey about public places, not a share of Shorts viewers ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tjmccue/2019/07/31/verizon-media-says-69-percent-of-consumers-watching-video-with-sound-off/)). The compromise (inference) is the existing short phrase-group caption, broken at phrase boundaries, with at most one word per group taking the highlight colour, and the same word highlighted in the table or code card at the same moment. Netflix caps reading speed at 20 characters per second ([Netflix Timed Text Style Guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)); a third-party summary gives a minimum event length of 5/6 second, about 25 frames, so shorter groups should be merged ([Gotham Lab](https://www.gothamlab.com/netflix-subtitle-delivery-requirements-complete-guide/)). Stress is shown by colour or a weight step, never by scaling or lifting the word.

## Prioritised actions for this setup

Priority runs from highest expected gain per unit of effort to lowest. The grade describes the evidence that the action helps, not the certainty that the implementation works; none of it has been run.

| # | Action | Evidence grade | Notes for this setup |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Write per-word timings in the voiceover step: Kokoro `start_ts`/`end_ts`; VITS `durations` from `inference()`; forced alignment of the final WAV for Chatterbox. Store one `Caption`-shaped JSON per scene WAV with the WAV's hash | Strong that the data exists (source code); inference for the file design | Feeds `@remotion/captions`, already installed at 4.0.529. No extra model for Kokoro or VITS |
| 2 | Cue the named element on its word: resolve marked words to frames, start the cue 2 frames early and never more than 1 frame late, and change nothing else at that moment | Strong for signalling and contiguity; the frame window is inference from a lip-sync standard | One cue per idea; the effect fades with excessive cueing |
| 3 | Resolve the entrance conflict: replace fade-and-rise and 0.96-scale entrances with fade, colour, masked wipe, line-draw and dimming, or get an explicit brand exception | Inference (brand compliance) | Decide before building new scenes; confirm whether camera push-ins are acceptable |
| 4 | Remove motion that carries no information, hold still during spoken pauses, and keep at most three things changing at once | Strong (coherence, decorative animation) | Matches the existing "every animation needs a job" review |
| 5 | Add a calm register for routine reveals (5 to 12 frames) and reserve the brand curve at 12 to 21 frames for hook, stressed word and payoff | Platform/practitioner (Carbon, Material) plus inference for the mapping | Needs a versioned change to the motion-review duration policy |
| 6 | Scale intensity along the arc with tempo, stagger and violet saturation only | Strong for direction (speed and saturation raise arousal); the arc is inference | No path distortion, shake or scale |
| 7 | Keep phrase-group captions, drive them from word timings, highlight one key word per group and the matching on-screen element together; merge groups shorter than about 25 frames | Strong for short keywords over full sentences; platform/practitioner for retention; inference for the pairing | Colour or weight only |
| 8 | Open with the question or result in text and voice inside 3 seconds, with motion already in progress at frame 1, and pay it off later | Platform/practitioner (TikTok ad research) | Treat hook type as an A/B variable |
| 9 | Rewrite scripts in conversational second person and apply the pause and speed settings from the earlier voice report | Strong for conversational wording (d about 0.8); voice-quality effect uncertain for neural TTS | Larger effect than any presenter option |
| 10 | Build each video as six to ten one-idea segments, with a camera move or cue marking each boundary | Strong for segmenting; segment count is inference | Fits the existing 2.5 to 6 s scene rule |
| 11 | Keep music low or absent under dense explanation; use a sound effect only to mark a meaningful event | Strong that irrelevant audio hurts learning; event-synced effects are untested inference | Platform research still says sound matters for attention |
| 12 | Measure a few sentences of Kokoro and VITS timings against a forced aligner to find any constant offset, then fold it into the lead | Inference | For Bangla, check the CC-BY-NC aligner licence first |
| 13 | Track viewed-versus-swiped and engaged views against the channel's own 60-second videos; test CTA length | Platform/practitioner | No published thresholds exist |
| 14 | Optional: a heavily smoothed glow on the active element driven by voice loudness, or the logo bars as a speaker mark on the subtitle bar | Inference only; no supporting study | Risk of rapid flashing; skip unless reviews show a lifeless frame |
| 15 | Do not add a talking head. If a character is wanted later, use a flat 2D figure that points, with Rhubarb mouth shapes | Strong (image principle null, gaze cost, 2D over 3D) | Realistic avatars need a GPU and trigger disclosure |

## Conclusion

The research moves the question from "how do we animate more" to "who decides when something moves". Today the scene clock decides; the evidence says the spoken word should. Once word timings exist as data, emphasis, captions, cues and pauses all become functions of the script, and the script becomes the single place where delivery is directed for voice and picture together. That also explains why a face adds so little: the feeling of a person explaining comes from visuals that behave like a pointing hand, arriving exactly as each thing is named.

The plan's largest unknown is that nobody has measured word-accurate cueing against scene-level timing on watch-through, in any format. The first two or three videos built this way are therefore the experiment. Comparing their viewed-versus-swiped and engaged-view figures with earlier 60-second videos on the same channel will show whether learning-science gains translate into feed retention, which no published source can yet say.
