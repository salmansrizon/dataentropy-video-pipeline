# Script and pauses humanise neutral voices

Neither current voice can be told to sound emotional: Kokoro-82M accepts only text, a voice tensor and a speed value, and the EMTIAZZ Bangla VITS is a single-speaker model with no style input, so genuine emotion (excitement, warmth on a chosen word) needs a different model. What can be done today is to raise both voices to a well-paced, correctly stressed, natural-sounding narrator. For Kokoro that means writing for the ear, using punctuation and misaki stress and phoneme markup, varying speed per line, and auditioning an `af_heart`/`af_bella` blend. For Bangla it means running the model locally instead of through the Hugging Face Space, because the three VITS knobs that govern liveliness, rhythm and speed cannot be reached through the Space, and because the model silently drops every Latin letter and ASCII digit. Pause timing is the best-evidenced polish step: one controlled study found naturalness peaks at about 0.6 s pauses and collapses at 0.15 s or less. Evaluation has to be layered, because automatic MOS predictors mostly measure signal cleanliness and none is trained on Bengali. A text-only judgment model can classify bucketed measurements (pitch variation, pause distribution, speaking rate, ASR error words, loudness), but a blind A/B listening test must outrank it. On licences, the Bangla model's weights are Apache-2.0 but its training recordings are undocumented, and the notes did not capture Kokoro's licence string, so both need a check before publication. Nothing in this report was listened to or run end to end; every setting below is a starting point to audition.

## Kokoro has no emotion control, so the script carries the expression

The Kokoro inference call is `model(ps, pack[len(ps)-1], speed)`: a phoneme string, one style vector and one speed float, with **no emotion, pitch, energy, SSML or style-prompt argument** ([kokoro/pipeline.py](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/blob/main/kokoro/pipeline.py)). The model is a decoder-only StyleTTS 2 variant with "no encoder release", so a new style cannot be derived from a reference recording either ([Kokoro-82M model card](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M)). A request for emotional features is open with no maintainer reply ([hexgrad/kokoro issue #254](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/issues/254)). Bracket tags such as `[pause:1.5s]` belong to the Kokoro-FastAPI wrapper, not to `KPipeline` ([Kokoro-FastAPI README](https://github.com/remsky/Kokoro-FastAPI)). The realistic ceiling is a pleasant neutral narrator; the levers that exist are all indirect.

**Line length is the strongest of them.** The style vector is selected by the phoneme count of the chunk, and the author documents a "goldilocks range" of 100–200 tokens, "weakness on short utterances, especially less than 10-20 tokens", and rushing above 400, with bundling short utterances as the suggested mitigation ([VOICES.md](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/main/VOICES.md)). The researchers estimate a 10–14 word line at roughly 50–90 phoneme tokens, which sits above the weak zone but below the sweet spot; that estimate was not measured on this project's scripts. The practical consequences are to avoid lines under about five words, attach punchy fragments to a neighbour inside the same string, and test synthesising two adjacent lines as one utterance and cutting at the sentence boundary with the per-token `start_ts`/`end_ts` timestamps the pipeline returns ([kokoro/pipeline.py](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/blob/main/kokoro/pipeline.py)). A newline inside a line must be avoided, because the default `split_pattern=r'\n+'` turns it into two independent short utterances.

**Punctuation and stress markup are the only intonation inputs the author advertises**: "To adjust intonation, try punctuation `;:,.!?—…"()“”` or stress `ˈ` and `ˌ`" ([Kokoro-TTS Space app.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/hexgrad/Kokoro-TTS/blob/main/app.py)). misaki accepts `[word](/phonemes/)` for exact pronunciation and `[word](-2…+2)` for stress, and treats ALL-CAPS words as stress +2; a word that already carries primary stress cannot be raised further, and a mistyped feature is ignored silently ([misaki/en.py](https://github.com/hexgrad/misaki/blob/main/misaki/en.py)). Emphasis therefore comes from demoting the neighbouring function words, raising a short contrast word such as `[not](+2)`, and placing the payload word last before a comma, dash or full stop. Phoneme markup is the reliable fix for brand and technical terms, using misaki's own inventory in which `A`, `I`, `O` replace the IPA diphthongs ([EN_PHONES.md](https://github.com/hexgrad/misaki/blob/main/EN_PHONES.md)). Two text traps deserve a guard in the build script: a bare four-digit integer is read as a year, and out-of-dictionary words are skipped when espeak-ng is missing, which on Windows needs the `.msi` install ([misaki/en.py](https://github.com/hexgrad/misaki/blob/main/misaki/en.py); [kokoro README](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro)).

**Speed is a uniform tempo scale, not an expressiveness control**: predicted durations are divided by `speed` before pitch is predicted ([kokoro/model.py](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/blob/main/kokoro/model.py)). Varying it per line still mimics how people talk; the researchers propose 0.90–0.95 for hook and outcome lines, 0.95–1.0 for dense explanation, and 0.85–0.9 for the single most important line, with adjacent lines within about 0.1 of each other. No source measures a benefit, so this is a listening experiment.

**Blending is supported but adds no emotion.** `af_heart` is the only A-grade voice and `af_bella` (A-) the only one near it; the grades describe training-data quality and quantity, not expressiveness ([VOICES.md](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/main/VOICES.md)). A comma-separated voice string is an equal-weight mean, and any FloatTensor can be passed as `voice`, so a weighted `0.7 * a + 0.3 * b` mix works offline ([kokoro/pipeline.py](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/blob/main/kokoro/pipeline.py)). Because columns `[:128]` of the style vector feed the decoder and `[128:]` feed the duration and pitch predictor ([kokoro/model.py](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/blob/main/kokoro/model.py)), keeping `af_heart`'s timbre while borrowing `af_bella`'s delivery is possible in principle. No listening result for that half-swap exists, so it is an experiment. Kokoro is deterministic for identical input, which makes an A/B harness over the same twelve lines cheap and repeatable.

## The Bangla model's expressive knobs are unreachable through the Space

Coqui VITS has three inference parameters: `inference_noise_scale` (default 0.667, variation in the sampled voice latent), `inference_noise_scale_dp` (default 0.8, variation in predicted durations) and `length_scale` (default 1, larger is slower) ([Coqui VITS docs](https://docs.coqui.ai/en/latest/models/vits.html)). They are model attributes read from the config, not per-call arguments ([vits.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/idiap/coqui-ai-TTS/dev/TTS/tts/models/vits.py)). The author's Space builds a `Synthesizer` with `use_cuda=False` and exposes **one text box and nothing else** ([Space app.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/EMTIAZZ/bangla-vits-tts/raw/main/app.py)). The model ships with `inference_noise_scale_dp` at 1.0, above the library default ([model config.json](https://huggingface.co/EMTIAZZ/bangladeshi-bangla-tts-vits/raw/main/config.json)), so uneven pacing in the Space output plausibly improves by lowering it toward 0.8. This is an inference, not a tested result.

Running locally is the unlock. The Space proves the checkpoint runs on CPU, and only `pytorch_model.pth` and `config.json` are needed. The original `TTS` 0.22.0 requires Python below 3.12 and is unmaintained ([PyPI TTS](https://pypi.org/project/TTS/)); the maintained fork `coqui-tts` 0.27.5 supports Python 3.10–3.14 but no longer bundles PyTorch ([PyPI coqui-tts](https://pypi.org/project/coqui-tts/)). Whether this checkpoint loads in the fork on Windows was not tested and needs a smoke test. Once local, the knobs are set by editing the config copy or assigning to `synthesizer.tts_model`. The proposed audition grid is noise scale 0.5 / 0.667 / 0.8, duration noise 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.0 and length scale 1.0 / 1.1. VITS output is stochastic, so three to five seeds per line should be rendered and the best take kept, with the seed recorded. These knobs add variation, not intent: nothing makes a chosen word emphatic.

**Text normalisation matters as much as the knobs.** The model is character-based with no phonemizer and no text cleaner, and its punctuation set is `.?-!|, ` ([model config.json](https://huggingface.co/EMTIAZZ/bangladeshi-bangla-tts-vits/raw/main/config.json)). Coqui discards any out-of-vocabulary character with only a console message and records it in `not_found_characters` ([tokenizer.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/coqui-ai/TTS/v0.22.0/TTS/tts/utils/text/tokenizer.py)). Latin "AI" or "RAG" and digits such as "2026" therefore vanish from the audio. The spoken text must write numbers as Bangla words, transliterate English terms into Bangla script as pronounced, and replace quotes, colons and dashes with commas or full stops. This fits the `tts` field the project already keeps separate from on-screen text. Two points are unresolved and need a local test: the exact vocabulary (the fetched character list came back garbled) and whether the danda "।" is encoded, dropped or needed as a sentence ender. `Synthesizer.tts()` also inserts a fixed 10,000-sample gap between sentences ([synthesizer.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/idiap/coqui-ai-TTS/dev/TTS/utils/synthesizer.py)), so synthesising sentence by sentence and placing pauses in the timeline gives control that the Space cannot. The colloquial-writing advice in the notes (চলিত forms, 6–12 word sentences, commas only at breaths) is unsourced working guidance for a native speaker to validate.

## Pause timing has evidence; EQ and compression do not

The only step with controlled listening evidence is pausing. With pauses at punctuation digitally replaced in human recordings, **0.6 s received the highest naturalness scores**, 0.075–0.15 s and 2.4–4.8 s the lowest, and longer pauses lowered perceived speech rate ([Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.778018/full)). Corpus work puts normal read-speech pauses in a 200–1000 ms band ([Campione & Véronis 2002](http://sprosig.org/sp2002/pdf/campione-veronis.pdf)). That study used 21–32 s textbook passages of human speech, not short-form TTS, so the researchers adapt it to roughly 0.2–0.35 s at commas, 0.4–0.7 s at sentence ends and up to about 1 s at scene changes. Both projects already generate one line per scene, so these gaps are set in the timeline, and inserted pauses count against the 135–155 wpm target.

For the rest of the chain, no source was found showing that EQ, compression, de-essing or reverb makes synthetic speech sound more human; the settings come from voiceover tutorials by analogy. Neural TTS has no room rumble or uneven performance level to fix, and compression above about 3:1 would flatten the intensity variation used below as an expressiveness signal. One machine-specific fact shapes what is possible: per the researchers' local check on 2026-10-07, the Remotion-bundled ffmpeg offers `loudnorm`, `volume`, `amix`, `adelay`, `apad`, `atempo` and `silencedetect` but no `highpass`, `equalizer`, `acompressor`, `deesser` or `sidechaincompress`. Loudness normalisation, a static music level and pause measurement are therefore available now; EQ would need the already-installed `scipy.signal` or a full ffmpeg build. `loudnorm` supports a two-pass linear mode fed by its own measurements ([FFmpeg filters documentation](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#loudnorm)). **-14 LUFS integrated with a -1 to -1.5 dBTP ceiling** is the one target every blog source accepts across YouTube, Reels and TikTok; none cites platform documentation, and the sources disagree on whether a hotter master helps on TikTok ([Audio Forge Pro](https://audioforgepro.com/blog/instagram-reels-vs-youtube-shorts-audio-comparison); [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-loudness-normalizers)). Music-under-voice advice ranges from 6–12 dB to 18–25 dB below speech ([Zella](https://zellahq.com/blog/music-ducking-explained/)); a fixed gain near 18–20 dB under near-continuous narration is the researchers' starting point.

## A text-only judge needs bucketed measurements and a human override

A judgment model that sees only scripts and numbers cannot assess delivery; it can classify measurements against declared bands, which matches the pattern the project already uses for motion review (questions to the model, policy and thresholds in code, main-agent confirmation). The value lies in making each measurement honest about what it proves.

**Automatic MOS predictors are an artefact check, not a naturalness verdict.** A 2026 probing study on 860 linguist-annotated utterances concluded MOS predictors "collapse onto acoustic signal quality" ([arXiv 2608.09930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09930)). Zero-shot on French TTS, the best system reached utterance-level SRCC of only 0.552, predictions cluster in the 3–4 range, and the authors warn of struggles with "language-dependent flaws like incorrect prosody" ([Kunešová et al., Interspeech 2024](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/kunesova24_interspeech.pdf)). No predictor in the notes is trained on Bengali. The lowest-friction options are `utmos22_strong` through `torch.hub`, MIT-licensed ([tarepan/SpeechMOS](https://github.com/tarepan/SpeechMOS)), and UTMOSv2, MIT, Python 3.9–3.13 ([UTMOSv2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sarulab-speech/UTMOSv2/main/README.md)). NISQA's weights are CC BY-NC-SA and should be avoided for company work ([NISQA](https://github.com/gabrielmittag/NISQA)). The sound use is relative: same script, dry voice track, candidate versus the current production clip.

**Pitch variation is the best-supported expressiveness number.** High pitch variation correlates with perceived liveliness and charisma, and speech without it is recalled worse and disliked ([Hincks & Edlund 2009](https://www.isca-archive.org/slate_2009/hincks09_slate.pdf)). Published values depend so heavily on tracker, window and octave errors that the same paper's "monotone" speakers measured about 5 semitones while whole-recording studies put ordinary speech near 3 ([F0 statistics and forensic phonetics](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251306660_Preliminary_F0_statistics_and_forensic_phonetics)). Buckets must therefore be calibrated on a human narrator clip run through the same code, one English and one Bangla, and expressed as a ratio to that reference. These numbers measure the amount of variation, never whether it falls on the right word.

**ASR round-trip catches dropped and garbled words, with a hard Bengali caveat.** faster-whisper runs on CPU with `compute_type="int8"` and needs no system ffmpeg ([faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)). Stock Whisper through large-v2 scores about **100% WER on Bengali** ([Whisper paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04356)), and un-fine-tuned large-v3 scores 55.5% normalised WER on Common Voice Bengali against 9.65% WER and 4.88% CER after fine-tuning ([mozilla-ai/whisper-large-v3-bn](https://huggingface.co/mozilla-ai/whisper-large-v3-bn)). Bengali checks therefore need a fine-tuned checkpoint and CER; that checkpoint's licence and CPU speed were not checked. For Bangla this check doubles as the detector for silently dropped Latin text. The judge should receive the list of differing words, not just a rate.

The table gives the researchers' proposed bands. Every edge is a proposal to calibrate in shadow mode, not a sourced threshold.

| Measurement (tool) | Proposed buckets | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking rate, wpm (existing `npm run srt`) | slow <130; target 135–155; fast >165 (English only; no Bangla norm found) | Pace, including inserted pauses |
| Pauses (`silencedetect`, or word timestamps) | clipped <0.15 s at punctuation; natural 0.15–0.5 s at commas and 0.3–1.0 s at sentence ends; disruptive >2 s; flags for hierarchy and mid-phrase pauses | Phrasing matches the script's punctuation |
| F0 SD in semitones, voiced frames, dry track | ratio to human reference: below <0.7; human-like 0.7–1.3; above >1.3; spread across sentences near zero = repeated contour | Amount of pitch movement, not appropriateness |
| Intensity SD in dB, before compression | ratio to human reference, same bands | Dynamic variation |
| ASR error (faster-whisper + `jiwer`) | English WER: clean 0–2%, minor 2–5%, problem 5–15%; Bangla CER with fine-tuned model: clean <5%, minor 5–10%, problem 10–20%; always pass the wrong-word list | Words are recognisable; not naturalness |
| Predicted MOS (UTMOS / UTMOSv2) | delta vs production baseline: worse (drop >0.3), same, better | Signal cleanliness only; low weight for Bangla |
| Loudness (`loudnorm print_format=json`) | integrated: quiet <-16, on target -15 to -13, loud >-12 LUFS; true peak ok ≤ -1.0 dBTP | Delivery compliance |
| Blind A/B result | prefers A/B when the sign test passes; no preference otherwise | The only direct evidence of "sounds human" |

The human test should copy the arena design: two anonymous clips of the same text, vote, reveal names afterwards ([TTS Arena blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/arena-tts)). For one listener that means loudness-matched pairs under randomised neutral filenames, one forced-choice question ("which sounds more like a person explaining this?"), 12–20 pairs, and a two-sided sign test, which needs 10 of 12 or 15 of 20 for p < 0.05. A native Bengali speaker is necessary for the Bangla clips. When this result disagrees with any automatic number, it wins, because those metrics are documented to miss prosody.

## Real emotion requires a model swap, and licences narrow the field

For English on a CPU, the Chatterbox family is the strongest fit on paper. It is MIT-licensed and supports CPU; Nano (110M) runs "3x faster than realtime on 8 CPU cores", and the 500M original is the only variant with an `exaggeration` knob, with the official tip of `cfg_weight` near 0.3 and `exaggeration` 0.7 or higher for expressive speech ([resemble-ai/chatterbox](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox)). Turbo and Nano drop that knob for speed ([HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox-turbo/discussions/22)). Every Chatterbox output carries an inaudible PerTh watermark, and the voice is pinned with a roughly 10 s reference clip, which must be a recording DataEntropy has rights to. Qwen3-TTS is Apache-2.0 and takes a natural-language `instruct` with preset English speakers Ryan and Aiden ([Qwen3-TTS card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-CustomVoice)); its CPU speed (RTF about 3–6.5, around 9 GB RAM) comes from unverified community reports. No measured CPU figure exists for Chatterbox original or Turbo. No verified listener ranking covers these candidates, and the Hugging Face Open TTS Leaderboard, which measures intelligibility rather than human-likeness, places Kokoro among the English WER leaders ([Open TTS Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-tts-leaderboard)). The upgrade case is expressiveness only, and it must be settled by auditioning the real script.

For Bangla the options are thinner. The same author's Orpheus 3B fine-tune is rated above the VITS and is tagged Apache-2.0, but it is a LoRA on a gated Llama base, recommends a 16 GB+ GPU, and its tags are non-verbal sounds rather than tone controls ([Orpheus Bangla card](https://huggingface.co/EMTIAZZ/orpheus-3b-bangla-high-data-finetuning)). Indic Parler-TTS is Apache-2.0, gated, and controls Bengali delivery through a description prompt with twelve emotions, at 0.9B parameters ([indic-parler-tts card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-parler-tts)); its accent is likely Indian rather than Bangladeshi, which is an inference from its speaker names. Azure offers two bn-BD neural voices with no speaking styles, so only `prosody` rate, pitch and volume apply ([Azure language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=tts)). ElevenLabs grants commercial use on paid plans, but its two documentation pages disagree on Bengali support ([ElevenLabs capabilities](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech); [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)).

## Conclusion

The research shifts the problem from "which settings add emotion" to "which layer owns expression". With these two models, expression lives in the script and the timeline, not in the synthesiser: Kokoro's delivery is a deterministic function of text length, punctuation and stress marks, and the Bangla VITS offers randomness rather than intent. That makes the writing step the real voice-direction step, and it makes the Bangla pipeline's current weakness mostly an integration problem, since the Space hides the knobs and swallows unsupported characters without warning.

It also means evaluation can never be fully delegated to numbers. Every automatic measure available here is either blind to prosody or blind to Bengali, so the judgment model's proper role is gatekeeper for regressions and compliance, with blind listening as the arbiter. If listening tests show the tuned neutral voices still fall short, the licence-safe upgrade path is narrow but real: Chatterbox or Qwen3-TTS for English on this PC, and for Bangla a choice between expressiveness on a rented GPU and reliability from a hosted Bangladeshi voice.
